# Source Grounding

## Evidence classes

### Observation
A result directly reported by the source: measured, observed, calculated, fitted, or otherwise explicitly produced by the study.

### Author interpretation
An explanation, mechanism, or conclusion explicitly proposed by the authors. Do not strengthen it into an established fact.

### Synthesis candidate
A relationship that may become meaningful only after comparison with already integrated sources. Keep it provisional until reconciliation.

### Agent inference
A plausible conclusion that the source does not explicitly establish. Do not integrate it as fact.

## Attribution

Every integrated scientific claim must remain traceable to each source that supports it, with source identity and relevant PDF page or page range when available. A newly encountered source that confirms an existing proposition contributes new evidence and must not be discarded as a duplicate claim. A one-sentence result from a distinct measurement method or evidence domain can be important and should be preserved. Paraphrase rather than copying long passages. Preserve separate source-specific conditions and attribution where they matter.

## Contradictions

When the current paper disagrees with existing evidence, preserve the actual result and conditions; do not average results or pick a winner merely because one source is newer. Explanations for disagreement must be attributed or labeled as inference.
